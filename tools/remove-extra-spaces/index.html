      Clean up messy text in one step: collapse double spaces, trim the ends of lines, remove blank lines or join lines into a paragraph, and strip invisible characters that cause problems.

      About the Remove Extra Spaces

      Text copied from PDFs, emails, spreadsheets and websites often carries stray spaces, tabs and broken line breaks. Choose the clean-up options you want and the result updates instantly, with a count of how many characters were removed.

      How to use the Remove Extra Spaces

      1. Paste the text you want to clean into the first box.
      2. Tick the clean-up options you need. The default options fix the most common spacing problems.
      3. Check the cleaned text in the result box — it updates as you change options.
      4. Copy the cleaned text or download it as a .txt file.

      Example use cases

      • Fixing text copied from a PDF where every line ends with a hard line break.
      • Removing double spaces after full stops in older documents.
      • Cleaning data pasted from spreadsheets before importing it elsewhere.
      • Stripping invisible non-breaking or zero-width spaces that break searches and code.
      • Tidying up email text or website content before publishing.

      Frequently asked questions

      What are non-breaking and zero-width spaces?

      A non-breaking space looks like a normal space but stops a line from breaking at that point. Zero-width spaces are invisible characters. Both often come from websites and word processors and can cause problems in searches, spreadsheets and code. The “fix” option turns non-breaking spaces into normal spaces and removes zero-width characters.

      How do I fix text copied from a PDF?

      Turn on “Remove line breaks” to join the broken lines into one paragraph. If your text has several paragraphs, process them one at a time, or keep line breaks on and edit manually.

      Will this change the words in my text?

      No. Only whitespace characters are changed or removed. Letters, numbers and punctuation stay exactly as they were.

      Does it work with code?

      It can, but be careful: indentation is significant in languages such as Python and YAML. Turn off “Trim spaces” and “Convert tabs” if you need to keep indentation.
