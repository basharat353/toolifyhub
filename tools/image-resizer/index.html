      Image Resizer

      Change an image’s width and height in pixels, or scale it by a percentage. Lock the aspect ratio to avoid stretching, pick an output format, and download the resized image.

      Original:

      Runs in your browser — your images are never uploaded or stored.

      About the Image Resizer

      Websites, social networks and online forms often require images at specific sizes. This resizer uses high-quality step-by-step downscaling for sharp results, and all processing happens privately in your browser.

      How to use the Image Resizer

      1. Upload an image by clicking the upload area or dragging a file onto it.
      2. Choose By pixels and enter a new width or height, or choose By percentage and enter a scale such as 50%.
      3. Keep “Keep aspect ratio” ticked to prevent stretching, and choose an output format if you want to change it.
      4. Click Resize image, check the preview, then download the result.

      Example use cases

      • Creating profile pictures, banners and thumbnails at the exact size a platform requires.
      • Shrinking large camera photos for faster websites and smaller email attachments.
      • Meeting the pixel requirements of job application, visa or exam upload forms.
      • Preparing product images with consistent dimensions for an online shop.
      • Scaling icons and graphics for presentations and documents.

      Frequently asked questions

      Will resizing reduce the image quality?

      Making an image smaller keeps it sharp — this tool downscales in several steps for a cleaner result. Making an image larger than its original size cannot add detail, so upscaled images may look soft.

      What does “keep aspect ratio” do?

      It keeps the width and height in the same proportion as the original, so the picture is not stretched or squashed. When you change one value, the other updates automatically.

      What is the largest size I can create?

      Up to 16,384 pixels on each side, though very large images may fail on phones and tablets because of browser memory limits.

      Are animated GIFs supported?

      You can resize a GIF, but only the first frame is kept, because the browser’s canvas cannot encode animations. The output is saved as PNG unless you choose another format.

      Is my image uploaded?

      No. Resizing happens in your browser using the Canvas API. Your image is never sent to a server.
