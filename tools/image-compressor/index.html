      Image Compressor

      Make images smaller for websites, email and uploads. Choose a quality level and optional maximum width, compare the result side by side, and download the compressed file.

      Lower quality = smaller file. 60–80% is a good balance for photos.

      About the Image Compressor

      Large photos slow down web pages and often exceed upload limits. This compressor re-encodes your image as JPEG or WebP using your browser’s built-in image engine. Your image is processed on your own device and is never uploaded to a server.

      How to use the Image Compressor

      1. Click the upload area or drag and drop a JPG, PNG or WebP image onto it.
      2. Adjust the Quality slider. The preview and file size update automatically.
      3. Optionally choose WebP for even smaller files, or set a maximum width to shrink large photos.
      4. Compare the original and compressed versions, then click Download compressed image.

      Example use cases

      • Speeding up a website by reducing the size of photos and banners.
      • Making photos small enough to attach to an email or upload to a form with a size limit.
      • Optimising product images for an online store.
      • Reducing phone photos before sharing them or storing them in the cloud.
      • Preparing images for a blog post without installing photo-editing software.

      Tips for the best results

      • Photos usually compress well at 60–80% quality with little visible difference.
      • Resizing helps most. A 4000-pixel-wide phone photo shown at 1200 pixels wastes data — set a max width to cut file size dramatically.
      • WebP files are typically smaller than JPEG at similar quality and are supported by all modern browsers.
      • Screenshots, logos and graphics with text may look blurry as JPEG. For these, a PNG is often a better choice.
      • Compression re-encodes the image, which removes most metadata such as camera details and GPS location.

      Frequently asked questions

      Are my images uploaded to your server?

      No. Compression uses your browser’s Canvas API, so the image never leaves your device. You can even disconnect from the internet after the page loads.

      Why is my compressed image larger than the original?

      If the original was already heavily optimised, re-encoding it at high quality can produce a larger file. Lower the quality, choose WebP, or set a smaller max width. The tool warns you when this happens.

      Can I compress PNG files without losing transparency?

      Choose WebP as the output format to keep transparency. JPEG does not support transparency, so transparent areas become white. Browsers cannot produce lossy PNG files, so PNG input is saved as JPEG or WebP.

      Does compression remove EXIF data such as location?

      Yes. Because the image is redrawn and re-encoded, the output normally contains no camera metadata or GPS coordinates. Most browsers apply the photo’s rotation before drawing, so orientation is kept.

      Can I compress several images at once?

      This tool handles one image at a time so you can fine-tune each result. To change formats in bulk, try the JPG to PNG or PNG to JPG converters, which accept up to 20 files.
