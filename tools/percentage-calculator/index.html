      About the Percentage Calculator

      Percentages come up everywhere — tips, test scores, pay rises, price changes and statistics. Pick the question that matches yours and enter the numbers.

      How to use the Percentage Calculator

      1. Find the calculator that matches your question, such as “What is X% of Y?”.
      2. Enter your numbers. Decimals and negative numbers are supported.
      3. Read the answer immediately below, along with the formula used.
      4. Use Clear all to start fresh.

      Example use cases

      • Working out a tip, a sales commission or VAT on a bill.
      • Turning a test score such as 42 out of 50 into a percentage.
      • Measuring growth in revenue, website traffic or prices between two periods.
      • Calculating a salary after a percentage pay rise.
      • Checking a reported percentage in a news article or report.

      Percentage formulas

      • X% of Y = (X ÷ 100) × Y. Example: 20% of 150 = 30.
      • X as a percentage of Y = (X ÷ Y) × 100. Example: 30 is 25% of 120.
      • Percentage change = ((new − old) ÷ |old|) × 100. Example: 80 to 100 is a 25% increase.
      • Increase by X% = value × (1 + X ÷ 100); decrease by X% = value × (1 − X ÷ 100).

      Note that a percentage increase followed by the same percentage decrease does not return to the start: 100 increased by 10% is 110, but 110 decreased by 10% is 99.

      Frequently asked questions

      What is the difference between percentage change and percentage points?

      If an interest rate goes from 2% to 3%, it rose by 1 percentage point, but by 50% in percentage terms. Percentage points measure the simple difference between two percentages.

      Why can’t I calculate percentage change from zero?

      Percentage change divides by the starting value. Dividing by zero is undefined, so any change from 0 cannot be expressed as a percentage.

      How are results rounded?

      Results show up to six decimal places (four for percentages), with trailing zeros removed. Round further as needed for your purpose.

      Can I use negative numbers?

      Yes. For percentage change, the tool divides by the absolute value of the starting number, so a move from −50 to −25 is correctly shown as a 50% increase.
