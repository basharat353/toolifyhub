      About the QR Code Generator

      The QR codes made here are static: the content is stored directly in the code, so it works forever, doesn’t track scans and doesn’t depend on any service staying online. The code is generated in your browser — nothing you enter is uploaded.

      How to use the QR Code Generator

      1. Type or paste a URL or any text into the box. The QR code appears instantly.
      2. Choose a size and error correction level. Use a higher level if the code may get scratched or partly covered.
      3. Optionally change the colours. Keep a dark foreground on a light background with strong contrast.
      4. Scan the preview with your phone to test it, then download it as a PNG for screens or an SVG for print.

      Example use cases

      • Linking a printed flyer, poster, menu or business card to your website.
      • Sharing a long URL, booking page or form at an event or in a classroom.
      • Adding a link to product packaging or a shop window.
      • Sharing contact details, a short message or a reference code.
      • Encoding Wi-Fi details as WIFI:T:WPA;S:NetworkName;P:Password;; so guests can join by scanning.

      Frequently asked questions

      Do these QR codes expire?

      No. They are static QR codes, so the content is stored in the pattern itself. They will keep working as long as the link or text inside them is valid.

      Can I track how many people scan my QR code?

      Not with a static code. To measure scans, encode a link that includes campaign parameters (such as UTM tags) and check your website analytics.

      Which error correction level should I choose?

      Medium (15%) suits most uses. Choose High (30%) for codes that will be printed small, placed outdoors or partly covered by a logo. Higher levels make the pattern denser.

      How much data can a QR code hold?

      Up to 2,953 bytes at the Low error correction level, and less at higher levels. For reliable scanning, keep content short — a short URL works best.

      Should I download PNG or SVG?

      PNG works everywhere on screens and in documents. SVG is a vector format that stays sharp at any size, making it ideal for professional printing.

      Is “QR Code” a trademark?

      Yes. QR Code is a registered trademark of DENSO WAVE INCORPORATED. The QR code standard itself is open and free to use.
