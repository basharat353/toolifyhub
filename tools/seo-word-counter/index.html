        About the SEO Word & Character Counter

        Search engines don’t reward a particular word count or keyword density. What helps is a clear title, an accurate description and content that fully answers the reader’s question. Use these numbers as a guide to write naturally for people — not as targets to game.

        How to use the SEO Word & Character Counter

        1. Enter your page title and meta description to see their length and an approximate search result preview.
        2. Optionally add a focus keyword or phrase you want the page to be found for.
        3. Paste the main content of your page to see word count, reading time and readability.
        4. Review the checks and frequent-word tables, then revise your copy so it reads naturally and clearly describes the page.

        Example use cases

        • Writing titles and meta descriptions that are less likely to be cut off in search results.
        • Checking that a blog post clearly covers its main topic before publishing.
        • Spotting words you have repeated too often while editing.
        • Comparing the length and readability of your page with similar pages.
        • Reviewing client copy or product descriptions during an SEO audit.

        How the numbers are calculated

        • Title width is estimated by measuring the text in 20 px Arial, close to how desktop search results display titles. Titles wider than about 600 px are often truncated. Search engines may also rewrite titles.
        • Description length guidance of about 120–160 characters reflects how much text typically appears. Search engines may show different text if it better matches a search.
        • Reading ease uses the Flesch Reading Ease formula with an estimated syllable count. It is designed for English; scores above 60 are generally easy for most adults.
        • Frequent words exclude common words such as “the” and “and”. “Share” is the percentage of all words in the content.

        Frequently asked questions

        What is the ideal keyword density?

        There isn’t one. Search engines don’t use a target density, and repeating a keyword unnaturally can make content worse. Use the keyword where it fits naturally — usually in the title, the opening and a heading — and use related terms elsewhere.

        How long should a page title be?

        Aim for roughly 50–60 characters (about 600 pixels) so it is likely to display in full. Put the most important words first, and make each page’s title unique.

        How long should a meta description be?

        Around 120–160 characters is a common guideline. Write a clear, accurate summary that encourages the right people to click. A meta description does not directly affect rankings.

        How many words should a page have?

        As many as it takes to answer the reader’s question well. A recipe or definition may need 300 words; an in-depth guide may need 2,000. Length by itself is not a ranking factor.

        Is my content uploaded?

        No. All analysis happens in your browser, so you can safely check unpublished drafts.
